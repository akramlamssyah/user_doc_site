1Application Overview

1.1 What is BlendOre?

BlendOre is a Windows desktop application that predicts and measures ore dilution caused by blast movement. It builds a three-dimensional model of the rock about to be blasted, colours every cubic metre of it with its ore grade, simulates how the blast throws that rock, and then tells you where the ore has actually ended up — so that the dig polygons handed to the loader follow the ore, not the survey lines drawn before the blast.

The problem it addresses is simple to state and expensive to ignore. Grade-control polygons are surveyed before a blast. The blast then moves the muckpile by several metres, horizontally and vertically. If the loader digs to the pre-blast polygons, ore is sent to the waste dump and waste is sent to the mill. BlendOre quantifies that displacement and re-draws the polygons on the post-blast position of the ore.

Everything happens in one 3D scene: the topographic point cloud, the blast design, the ore geometry, the tetrahedral model, the simulated movement and the resulting dig polygons are all objects in the same tree, and every operation produces a new object you can inspect, measure, colour and export.

1.3 Business context

BlendOre is part of the EPC Groupe Simulation Tools Suite, developed by GTS (Global Technical Solutions). It shares its sign-in, licensing and settings infrastructure with the other tools in the suite, and authenticates against Explore, EPC's web platform.

The value the tool is built to deliver is the difference between ore recovered and ore mined. Every polygon BlendOre produces carries a volume, a tonnage, a mean grade, a waste percentage and a profit figure computed from your own ore price, processing cost, rock density and cut-off grade — so a change in the blast design or in the dig-polygon shape can be argued in dollars rather than in metres.

1.5 How BlendOre works

A complete study runs through six stages. Each produces an object in the tree that the next stage consumes.

  1. Import. A topographic point cloud of the bench, the blast design (hole collars, toes, diameters, loading plan, delays), and the ore geometry — either grade-control ore lines (.str) or a block model.
  2. Mesh. BlendOre builds a tetrahedral volume model of the rock between the topography and a floor elevation you choose, and writes the ore grade of every ore polygon or block onto the tetrahedra that fall inside it. This is the object everything downstream works on.
  3. Sequence. Optionally, design or edit the initiation timing — electronic templates or a NONEL connector network — and see the isochrones on the pattern.
  4. Simulate. The movement solver computes a blast pressure field from the loading plan and the delays, throws each tetrahedron along the direction perpendicular to the local isochrones, redistributes momentum between neighbouring elements, and lets the muckpile settle under gravity. The result is a displacement vector per tetrahedron.
  5. Assess. Slice the moved model into bench layers, cut dig polygons on each layer — by hand, automatically from the grade field, or from imported polygons — and read volume, tonnage, grade, dilution and profit per polygon.
  6. Report. Export the polygons as STR for the mine-planning package, and the analysis as PDF, XLSX or CSV.

BlendOre Plus adds a seventh stage that closes the loop: after the blast, load the surveyed post-blast surface and run Adjust Post Blast. It corrects the predicted displacement against reality and writes back calibrated values for initial pressure, friction and bulking, so the next prediction starts from measured behaviour rather than from defaults. Chapter 8 covers it.

2.2 Installing BlendOre

BlendOre is distributed as a single signed MSI named BlendOre-<version>.msi — for this release, BlendOre-0.9.6.msi.

  1. Double-click the MSI. No elevation prompt appears — the install is per user.
  2. On the install-location page, accept the default or choose your own. The default is %LOCALAPPDATA%\GTS-TOOL\BlendOre\.
  3. On the Additional options page — “Select whether you want a Desktop shortcut.” — leave Create a Desktop shortcut ticked (it is ticked by default) or clear it.
  4. Confirm and install. A Start-menu shortcut named BlendOre is created under GTS-TOOL\BlendOre.

Nothing is written outside the install folder until you first launch the application. On first launch BlendOre seeds its settings in the registry and creates a BlendORE_Output folder (section 12.4).

2.3 Updating

To update, download the newer BlendOre-<version>.msi and run it. It removes the previous version automatically and installs in its place. Your settings, grade colour bands, report authors and Explore session are stored separately and survive both an upgrade and an uninstall.

There is no in-application update check. BlendOre never tells you a newer version exists and has no “check for updates” command. Ask your site's technical contact, or watch for the release announcement.

Installing an older MSI over a newer install fails with the message Une version plus récente de BlendOre est déjà installée. Uninstall the newer version first if you genuinely need to roll back.

2.4 Signing in to Explore

BlendOre has no licence key: your entitlement comes from your Explore account. You sign in the first time you run it, and again each time the licence is revalidated — roughly once every week or two. Between those checks BlendOre starts without a network connection.

  1. Launch BlendOre. A splash screen with the edition logo appears briefly.
  2. The LOG IN dialog opens. Enter your Username / Email and Password. The eye button at the right edge of the password field reveals what you have typed.
  3. Click LOG IN.

BlendOre authenticates against Explore, then reads your group memberships to decide which edition to open. If you belong to exactly one BlendOre group, that edition starts immediately.

You need connectivity when the licence is revalidated. If Explore is unreachable at that moment you will not get past the login dialog, and cancelling it closes the application. On a site with intermittent connectivity, sign in while you have a link rather than waiting until you need the software.

4.2 Geometry

The topography is the foundation of the whole study: it defines the top surface of the 3D model and, after the blast, it is what the prediction is measured against.

Format How it is read
.csv .txt .xyz .asc Through the column mapper (section 4.7). You can map X, Y, Z, point intensity, R/G/B colour and a text label.
.obj Vertices and faces. If a companion .mtl and its texture files are found next to the OBJ, a textured surface is built and the display mode is forced to Surface; otherwise vertex colours are used.
.las X/Y/Z plus the LAS intensity field, which becomes the point scalar.
.dxf Points, lines, polylines, 3D faces, circle and arc centres, and block insertion points. Polylines are kept and drawn as lines.
.dtm Comma-delimited. Only rows with exactly eight fields are read, taking X, Y, Z from fields 2, 3 and 4.
.stl ASCII STL only. A binary STL loads as an empty object with no warning.

On a successful load the object appears in the tree named after the file, the Informations tab fills in the file name, path and vertex count, and the point cloud is drawn at point size 2.

Clouds over one million points open the downsample dialog automatically. This is deliberate: meshing and simulation on a raw drone cloud are impractically slow. Accept the suggested voxel size or set your own, press Compute to see the resulting count, then Apply. Section 11.7 describes the dialog.
Object names lose every extension. A file called bench_402.v2.csv appears in the tree as bench_402, and mesh.v2.vtu and mesh.v2.blnd both appear as mesh. Rename rows in the tree when you need to tell two objects apart.

4.3 Design

The design carries far more than hole positions: BlendOre needs the loading plan and the delays to compute a pressure field, so the richer the design file, the better the simulation.

JSON — the reference format

The native format, and the one BlendOre writes back on export. It carries, per hole: hole number, collar and toe coordinates, diameter in metres, hole length, an empty-hole flag, the list of loading decks with their products and masses, and the firing delays in milliseconds. Air decks and stemming are recognised as non-blasting material.

Excel (.xlsx)

One header row, then one row per hole. Columns are matched by name, not by position — the matcher is case-insensitive, ignores spaces, hyphens and underscores, folds accents, and recognises English and French aliases. Four columns are mandatory:

Role Typical headers accepted Error if missing
Hole number Hole N, hole number, hole id, blasthole, trou, numero trou… Colonne Hole Number introuvable.
Depth DEPTH, hole depth, PROFONDEUR, longueur forage… Colonne Depth/Profondeur introuvable.
Diameter Diameter, diametre, diam, calibre… Colonne Diamètre introuvable.
Number of decks nbSequence, sequence number, nombre de séquences… Colonne NbSequence introuvable.

Collar and toe coordinates are read as three consecutive columns following an anchor header such as Pos TOP Trous / collar and Pos BOT Trous / toe. Loading and timing are read as runs of consecutive columns from their own anchors: up to nine ScriptConfig columns, up to nine Chargement (product) columns and up to three Time Sequence columns.

Incomplete rows are reported. A hole is kept only if all four mandatory fields and all six coordinate columns are filled; BlendOre warns you when a hole could not be read, so you know the imported pattern is short. It is still worth checking the hole count in the Informations tab against your design and spot-checking a hole in Holes Info. before simulating, so that any hole whose loading plan came through empty is caught before it silently contributes no explosive.

CSV

Goes through the column mapper with the design role set: Hole ID, Depth, Diameter, Collar X/Y/Z, Toe X/Y/Z, NbSequence, ScriptConfig 1…9, Chargement 1…9 and Time Sequence 1…3. All six collar and toe columns are mandatory. If Hole ID is left unmapped, holes are numbered 1…N.

Selecting a role ending in 1 — ScriptConfig 1, Chargement 1 or Time Sequence 1 — automatically assigns the following roles to the following consecutive columns. Map the first of each block and the rest fills itself in.

After the design loads

Ore lines are the grade-control polygons: closed rings, one per ore body per bench level, each carrying an ore grade and usually a density. This is the most common way of getting ore geometry into BlendOre, and .str is the only ore-line format that carries grades.

How the file is read

Lines beginning with 0 separate one polygon from the next; every other line is a data row split on commas. A polygon's ore grade is the mean of its grade column and its density is the first density value found. Rows that cannot be parsed are skipped without a message.

Pipe-delimited STR files preview correctly and then load nothing. The preview table in the configuration dialog accepts both a comma and a pipe (|) as separators, but the reader itself accepts only commas. A pipe-delimited file therefore looks perfectly mapped in the dialog and produces an empty ore line, with no error. Convert it to comma-delimited first.

BlendOre then groups the polygons into bench levels by their mean Z and derives a layer height per level from the spacing between levels. This is what makes an ore line three-dimensional: each polygon becomes a prism, extruded up or down by its layer height (section 9.2).

5Building the 3D Ore Model

5.1 What the mesh is

Mesh 3D is the pivotal command in BlendOre. It converts a topographic surface and a set of ore geometry into a tetrahedral volume model of the rock about to be blasted: a solid bounded above by the topography, below by a floor elevation you choose, and laterally by the extent of the survey (or, when a design is selected, by a buffer around the blast pattern).

Every tetrahedron in that solid carries an ore grade, taken from the ore polygon or block it falls inside. This is the object that the movement solver throws, that layers are cut from, and that all KPIs are computed on. Nothing downstream works without it.

Conformal meshing. When ore geometry is present, BlendOre does not simply drape grades over an arbitrary mesh. It splits the topographic solid along every ore boundary, so ore/waste interfaces become real faces of the mesh rather than a stepped approximation. That is what makes the dilution figures meaningful.

5.2 Preconditions

1 GEO + 1 BLK Conformal ore mesh from the block model.
1 GEO + 1 BLS + 1 ORE Conformal ore mesh, clipped to a buffer around the blast pattern.
1 GEO + 1 BLS + 1 BLK As above, from the block model.

5.4 How grade is attached

Grade assignment is geometric containment, not geostatistical interpolation. BlendOre computes the centroid of every tetrahedron and asks, for each ore polygon or block in turn: is this centroid inside the polygon in plan, and inside its vertical slab?

  • The vertical slab of a polygon at elevation Z with height h is [Z−h, Z] downward, or [Z, Z+h] upward.
  • A block model block becomes a rectangle at its top face with the block's own height, always downward.
  • Tetrahedra inside no polygon keep grade 0.0 — they are waste.
Where polygons overlap, the last one processed wins. There is no averaging and no error. Overlapping ore lines at the same elevation therefore produce a result that depends on file order — clean them up in your grade-control package before exporting.

5.5 Reading the result

The new mesh appears as a child of the topography, named <topography name>_mesh_1 (then _2, _3…). It is coloured by Ore Grade using the bands from Settings › Thresholds / Colors, and the Ore KPI : pane fills with its volume, tonnage, mean grade, dilution and profit.

The mesh-scalars combo stays empty and disabled at this point. That is expected: a fresh ore mesh carries exactly one scalar, Ore Grade, and the combo only populates when there is more than one to choose between. A second scalar appears after Interpolate (section 5.6) or after a movement simulation.

Check the model before going further:

  1. Switch the view-mode combo to Surface with Edges and confirm the element size looks right for the bench.
  2. Set a non-zero cut-off in Configurate and press H to hide waste — the remaining shape should be recognisably your ore body.
  3. Compare Item Volume : and Item Mass : in the KPI pane against your own bench estimate.

If meshing fails

The conformal mesher refuses to produce a result it cannot trust rather than silently falling back to a plain mesh. The message names the cause; the common ones are:

Message contains Cause and fix
“no Ore Line polygon with a scalar strictly greater than the selected cutoff” Every polygon is at or below the cut-off. Lower Cutoff grade in the dialog.
“Ore Line polygon is self-intersecting or has negligible area” A bow-tie or degenerate ring in the STR. Repair it in your grade-control package.
“more than 6000 atomic regions” Too many overlapping levels at once. Load fewer bench levels, or simplify the contours.

6.6 Converting between the two

Both directions are available, and neither is destructive — you can always convert back.

NONEL → Electronic

The button → Electronic (Keep Timings) promotes the NONEL timings to an electronic sequence. This direction is exact: the delays are simply reinterpreted. Holes you left unconnected are assigned the mean delay of the connected ones, so the electronic field is fully defined.

Electronic → NONEL

The button → NonEL (Auto) works out a connector network that reproduces the electronic timing as closely as the standard connector delays allow. It builds a spatial neighbour graph, works through the holes in firing order, and for each one picks the parent and catalogue delay whose sum lands closest to the target time.

7.3 Choosing the time step

Delta Time (dt) sets the integration step of the solver, in milliseconds. Range 0.01–100 ms; BlendOre pre-fills a value computed from your own timing sequence, hole positions and diameters.

The trade-off is not just speed versus accuracy. The step must be short enough that two holes close enough to interact do not detonate within the same step, or the blast will behave as if they were simultaneous.

The Temporal study... button opens a dedicated window — Temporal study - time step (dt) selection — that makes this visible:

Panel Shows
Left, full height The pattern in plan, holes coloured by detonation time, with a line joining every spatially close pair. Pairs in conflict with the current dt are drawn in thick red.
Top right Nearby detonations per time step. Markers turn red where more than one nearby hole shares a step.
Bottom right A histogram of the time gaps between spatially close pairs, with the current dt marked in red dashed and the suggested dt in green dotted.

A summary line reads “<n> detonations | <n> nearby pairs | <n> pairs in conflict | <n> steps shared by >1 nearby detonation”. Drag the slider (2–50 ms) and watch the red lines appear and disappear.

Auto suggestion (<n> ms) picks the largest dt that stays coherent for the large majority of interacting pairs. It works from the gap distribution rather than the single smallest gap, so a handful of unusually tight pairs do not drag the whole simulation down. Apply this dt writes the value back into the movement dialog.

Start with the automatic suggestion. Reduce dt only if you have a specific reason — conflicts that matter for your study, or an unexpectedly coarse result. Halving dt roughly doubles the runtime.

8Post-Blast Control

This chapter documents the BlendOre Plus package. Post-blast control — correction against the surveyed muckpile, parameter re-calibration and post-blast QAQC — is provided by BlendOre Plus and is not part of the BlendOre package.

8.1 What it does

A prediction is only as good as its calibration. Adjust Post Blast closes the loop: it takes the muckpile you actually surveyed after the blast, compares it against the surface the simulation predicted, and does two things with the difference.

  1. Corrects the model. The surface discrepancy is propagated down into the tetrahedral volume, so the corrected model puts the ore where the measured surface says it is — and the dig polygons you cut from it are correspondingly better.
  2. Re-calibrates the physics. It works backwards from the discrepancy to adjust the initial pressure, the friction and the bulking factor, and writes them into your settings. Your next prediction on a similar bench starts from measured behaviour rather than from generic defaults.
This is the highest-value habit in BlendOre. One correction run after each shot builds a calibration for your rock, your explosive and your bench geometry. The gain compounds: predictions on the third bench are markedly better than on the first.

8.2 Preconditions

Select exactly two objects:

  • the MSH movement result — the _Move_Predict object, which must carry a displacement field;
  • the GEO surveyed post-blast point cloud.

Anything else gives Invalid selection / “Please select one mesh and one geo.”

Import the post-blast survey as an ordinary Geometry file (section 4.2), in the same coordinate system as the pre-blast topography. A photogrammetry or laser scan of 2–5 cm point spacing is the intended input.

8.4 Parameter re-calibration

Alongside the corrected model, BlendOre derives two relative errors from the surface discrepancy — one horizontal, one vertical — using median statistics over the elements that moved significantly, and updates three settings:

Parameter Adjusted Clamped to
Initial Pression (GPa) Up if the real movement exceeded the prediction, down if it fell short. —
Rock friction / damping (Pa.s-1) Inversely — more movement needed means less damping. 0.1–0.8
Compaction / Swell vertical factor (Z) From the vertical error — more heave means a higher bulking factor. 0.1–2.0

The adjustment is deliberately damped: a 10 % relative error moves a parameter by only about 5 %, and the per-run change is capped. That keeps a single anomalous shot from throwing the calibration off.

Validate a calibration before recalibrating. The intended cycle is: correct a shot → get new parameters → run a fresh prediction on the next bench with those parameters → correct that one. Running several corrections in a row without a prediction between them stacks adjustments derived from the same starting point.

The new values appear pre-filled the next time you open Calculate Movement. Note them down — with Save Traceability enabled they are recorded in each run's log, which is the easiest way to keep a calibration history.

9.2 Polygon orientation — get this right

9.3 Cutting a dig polygon by hand

Select a layer, then:

  1. Press E, or click Select Points. The camera snaps to top view and Cut Points appears.
  2. Left-click each vertex of the dig polygon. The Z of the first click is frozen so the ring stays horizontal, and the last vertex follows the cursor until you click again.
  3. Right-click to close the ring.
  4. Press C, or click Cut Points.

Middle-drag pans while you draw. A new object appears under the layer with its own KPIs, and selection mode switches itself off. Dig blocks are named Digblock_ followed by the block's base elevation and an internal id — for example Digblock_137.512 — so rename them in the tree if the report needs meaningful names.

You need at least three clicked vertices. With fewer you get Warning / “Please draw a selection polygon before cutting.”
Membership is decided in plan, on centroids. A tetrahedron belongs to the polygon if its centroid falls inside the ring in X and Y. The polygon's elevation is then forced to the layer's top or bottom according to the orientation setting — you cannot cut a sloping polygon.

11.4 Slice

Produces a thin cross-section through a mesh. It works exactly like Clip — same plane widget, same Origin / Normal fields, same Set plane values and Toggle plane buttons — but the result is a flattened section named <name>_sliced, carrying the ore grade of the cells it passes through.

A slice is a thin shell of the tetrahedra the plane crosses, flattened towards the plane — not a mathematically exact cross-section. It is intended for visual inspection of the grade distribution at depth, not for area calculations.

Converts scalars stored per tetrahedron into scalars stored per vertex, giving a smoothly shaded model rather than a faceted one. It runs immediately with no dialog and produces a child object named <name>_pointdata.

11.6 Rescale Data

Changes the range of the colour map without touching the data — the way to bring out detail in a narrow band of grades.

The dialog Rescale Data Range shows the data's original range and the current colour range, and offers Minimum Value : and Maximum Value : fields with Reset and Apply buttons.

Downsample data

Reduces a point cloud by voxel grid: the cloud is divided into cubes of the given size and one point is kept per occupied cube. Enabled for a single point cloud, and opened automatically for clouds over a million points on import.

11.8 Ruler

Measures a straight-line distance in the 3D scene. Tick Ruler — enabled as soon as anything is selected — and a Ruler group box appears in the Properties tab.

  2. Right-click the second point. A red line joins them and the distance is written at the midpoint.
  3. Right-click again to start a new measurement.

The dialog Histogram has a Number of classes : spin box (5–500, default 100) and two tabs:

15Troubleshooting & FAQ

15.1 Start-up and sign-in

Can I use BlendOre offline?
Yes, between licence checks. BlendOre revalidates your entitlement against Explore roughly once every week or two; you need network access to https://explore.epc-groupe.com/ at that moment, not at every launch. On a site with intermittent connectivity, sign in while you have a link.
My password is right but I get “The email address and password do not match.”
The same message appears when Explore cannot be reached at all. Open the Explore site in a browser from the same machine. If it does not load, the problem is the network or a proxy, not your credentials.
I signed in but get “Access denied”.
Your account is not in a BlendOre group. Ask your Explore administrator to add you to BlendOre plus or BlendOre Predict.
Why does my colleague have a “Post Blast Control” tab and I do not?
They are licensed for BlendOre Plus, a separate package that adds post-blast control on top of BlendOre. Section 1.4 compares the two.
My settings do not survive a restart.
Settings are written to the Windows registry and failures there are silent. Check with your IT team that BlendOre is allowed to write to the Windows registry under your user account. Note also that two options are reset deliberately every session: Save pre-movement simulation and Save post-movement simulation, and that Include heave in a separate layer always reverts to unticked.

15.2 Import problems

My CSV appears as a single column in the mapper.
The default delimiter is a semicolon. Click Comma (,) or Whitespace and the preview redraws immediately.
My block model will not load — “Could not read the CSV file.”
The file is not a readable CSV. Re-export it from your mine-planning package as CSV, and check the delimiter in the column mapper (section 4.7).
The block model loaded but sits half a block off.
The coordinate convention was mis-detected. Re-import with Choose colums manually and set the convention explicitly — A (centroid), B (lower corner) or C (regular grid, size inferred). The detection summary reports a reliability percentage; below 60 % it is worth checking by hand.
My multi-file STR load aborted.
All files in one selection must have the same column count and delimiter. Load the odd one separately.
My ore line has no grades.
Either the OreGrade column: number in the STR configuration is wrong — check the preview table — or you imported from CSV, TXT or DXF, which collapse to a single polygon with grade 0. Only .str carries per-polygon grades.
My design loaded but has fewer holes than it should.
Rows missing a mandatory field cannot be read, and BlendOre warns you when that happens. Fix the source file, or check the hole count in Informations and open Holes Info. to confirm the loading plan came through: a hole with no Type / Length rows carries no explosive.
Two objects that should overlap appear far apart.
A translation-vector mismatch (section 4.8). Compare their Translation : values in the Properties tab and correct one of them. Answering No to the translation prompt on import is the usual cause.

15.3 Meshing problems

Mesh 3D is greyed out.
The selection is wrong. For an ore mesh you need exactly one point cloud plus exactly one ore line or block model, optionally plus one design. A point cloud on its own, or a point cloud plus a design, also works but produces a mesh with no ore grade. Section 14.3 has the full list; check the badges on the selected rows.
“No Ore Line polygon with a scalar strictly greater than the selected cutoff.”
Every polygon is at or below the cut-off. Lower Cutoff grade in the Make Mesh dialog, or check that the grade column was read correctly.
Meshing takes far too long.
Raise the characteristic length — 2.5 m rather than 1.5 m makes a large difference. Downsample the point cloud. With a design selected, reduce Max Cutting Distance (m). For a sub-blocked block model, switch Meshing mode to Grade classes.
The mesh-scalars combo is empty after meshing.
Expected. A fresh ore mesh has one scalar, so there is nothing to choose between. A second appears after Interpolate or a movement simulation.
My ore body is one bench too high or too low.
The polygon orientation is wrong. See section 9.2 — and be aware the result looks entirely plausible, so this is worth verifying rather than assuming.

15.4 Simulation problems

Launch BlendORE Simulation is greyed out.
Select the mesh, the design and the ore geometry together; or, if the mesh already carries an Ore Grade scalar, just the mesh and the design.
“We are missing key design data: sequence, diameter, configuration…”
The design has no loading plan. Confirm in Holes Info. and re-import with the loading and timing columns mapped (section 4.3).
The simulation is very slow.
Runtime scales with element count and inversely with the time step. Use a coarser characteristic length, and use Temporal study... to confirm you are not running a smaller dt than the pattern requires.
The predicted movement looks too small or too large.
The three parameters that scale it are Initial Pression (GPa), Rock friction / damping and the Compaction / Swell factor. Rather than guessing, run Adjust Post Blast on a shot you have surveyed: it calibrates all three from measured data (chapter 8).
I lost my previous simulation results.
The output folder is named from the date and time to the minute plus the parameter values. Re-running with the same parameters inside the same minute deletes and recreates it. Change a parameter, or wait a minute.
Play is greyed out, or says no animation data.
Animation needs the velocity history. Select the original _Move_Predict object, not a layer or a derived object. If you reloaded from a .blnd, the data was only saved if you answered Yes to the animation-data question on export.

15.5 Greyed-out commands and other surprises

A command I need is greyed out.
Section 14.3 lists the required selection for every command. Hovering the button also shows its requirements in the tooltip.
Selecting one object makes another disappear.
Selection is visibility — only selected objects are drawn. Ctrl-click to show several at once.
Hide / Show Waste does nothing.
The cut-off grade is still 0.0, so nothing counts as waste. Set a real value in Configurate.
All my material is ore, and my dilution figures look wrong.
The cut-off grade is still 0.0. It drives recovery, profit, the waste toggle and the automated cut, all of which then treat everything as ore. Dilution is the subtle one: rather than going to zero it is computed against a hard floor of 0.01 g/T, so it returns a plausible-looking number that is not against your real cut-off. Set the cut-off in Configurate at the start of a study.
My exported STR loads with X and Y swapped.
BlendOre writes STR in Y, X, Z order. Adjust your import mapping; the header line in the file states the order.
A point cloud looks sparser than it should.
Either the Filter points by color slider in Rescale Data is hiding part of it, or the cloud was downsampled — automatically on import above a million points, or by you in place.
My layer colours changed after reopening a project.
Grade bands are not stored in the project. They come from the current Thresholds / Colors settings, which must have changed since you saved.
I need to undo something.
There is no undo. Deletions, downsampling and validated polygon edits are immediate and permanent. Save the project at the milestones of a study — after meshing, after simulating, after cutting — so you always have a point to return to.

16.1 Terms

Term Meaning in BlendOre
Blast contour The outline of the pattern in plan — the convex hull of all collars and toes, optionally buffered outward. Produced by Delimit polygons.
Blast site The object type given to a mesh once it has been through a movement simulation. Layers are cut from it.
Bulking / swell factor The vertical expansion of the muckpile. Above 1 the rock swells and heaves; below 1 it compacts.

Deck / level One charge or stemming segment within a hole. A hole with three decks has three detonator levels.
Digblock The object created by cutting a dig polygon out of a layer. Carries its own volume, tonnage, grade, dilution and profit.
Dig polygon A closed ring on a layer defining what the loader should dig as ore.
Dilution The proportion of a volume that is below the cut-off — waste that will be mined as ore. Shown as Waste (%) in reports.
Displacement field The per-tetrahedron movement vector produced by the simulation. The core output.
